# marche-regional-stats v0.2: design

Date: 2026-10-09. Status: approved in conversation, section by section; this document awaits review.

## Goal

Bring the content of the two v0.1 Power BI reports into the reproducible Python pipeline, fix their methodological weaknesses, and rebuild the Power BI report as a text-based project (`.pbip`) on top of the pipeline's output.

Audience: reviewers of applications for EU and official statistics roles. The project must show two things at once:

- sound statistical method (age standardisation, comparable indicators, uncertainty and reliability made explicit);
- Power BI skill (star schema, DAX measures, a calculation group) in a form that can be read on GitHub without Power BI.

Success criteria:

1. `marche-stats run` produces, from Eurostat data, the mortality, education and economy results in `docs/results.md` and the model tables in `data/model/`.
2. The Marche age-standardised death rate agrees with Eurostat's published rate (`hlth_cd_asdr2`) within 3 % in every year both exist.
3. The Power BI project opens after a clone and refreshes with no configuration, and its numbers match the Python results (documented cross-check).
4. CI is green: lint, unit tests, model contract, TMDL contract, Eurostat benchmark on the committed tables.

## Decisions taken

| Decision | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Overall approach | Python pipeline is the single source of numbers; Power BI is the presentation layer | Numbers cannot diverge between `results.md` and the report |
| Power BI format | `.pbip` with TMDL semantic model and PBIR report | Model, Power Query and DAX are readable and diffable on GitHub |
| Model file format | CSV, UTF-8, `.` as decimal separator | Readable on GitHub, no driver, no new dependency |
| `data/model/` | Committed to git | The report opens right after a clone; tables are small Eurostat-derived aggregates |
| Education content | Enrolment counts and three EU 2030 indicators | Counts alone are not comparable across regions |
| Old `.pbix` files | Removed from the tree, kept in tag `v0.1.0` | They carry the method issues v0.2 fixes |

## 2. Method

### Age-standardised mortality (`mortality.py`)

- **Standard population:** European Standard Population 2013 (ESP), five-year groups 0–4 to 95+, summing to 100,000. Population data end at the open group 85+, so the ESP weights for 85–89, 90–94 and 95+ are merged into one 85+ weight of 2,500.

  | Age | Weight | Age | Weight |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0–4 | 5,000 | 45–49 | 7,000 |
  | 5–9 | 5,500 | 50–54 | 7,000 |
  | 10–14 | 5,500 | 55–59 | 6,500 |
  | 15–19 | 5,500 | 60–64 | 6,000 |
  | 20–24 | 6,000 | 65–69 | 5,500 |
  | 25–29 | 6,000 | 70–74 | 5,000 |
  | 30–34 | 6,500 | 75–79 | 4,000 |
  | 35–39 | 7,000 | 80–84 | 2,500 |
  | 40–44 | 7,000 | 85+ | 2,500 |

- **Deaths:** `demo_r_magec` single years of age are summed into the same five-year groups. Deaths of unknown age (`UNK`) are redistributed in proportion to the known-age deaths of the same geography, year and sex; their share is reported in `results.md`.
- **Denominator:** average population of the year, the mean of the populations on 1 January of year t and t+1, as Eurostat does. The v0.1 provincial crude rate keeps its 1 January denominator; both choices are documented.
- **Rate:** direct standardisation, per 100,000, by sex (T, M, F).
- **Confidence interval:** 95 %, Dobson et al. (1991), using exact Poisson limits for the total deaths (`scipy.stats.chi2`).
- **Five-year rate:** replaces the v0.1 measure `Mortality_Rate_Last_5_Years_Weighted`. It is a pooled rate: deaths and populations are summed over the five years in each age group, then standardised. It is not an average of annual rates. The window is the latest five years with complete data (all 18 age groups) for the six regions and Italy; an area without complete data in every year of the window is left out and named in `results.md`, never pooled over fewer years.
- **Incomplete aggregates:** an area and year lacking deaths or population for any age group gets no standardised rate. In the October 2026 data the EU27 aggregate is complete only in 2021 and 2022 (for example, 1 January 2024 lacks the 30–34 and 45–49 groups), so EU27 rates appear only in those years and the EU27 is left out of the five-year table. Found by running the pipeline on real data on 2026-10-09.
- **Benchmark:** Eurostat's published standardised death rate (`hlth_cd_asdr2`, all causes, by sex) is stored next to ours. Small differences are expected, from the 85+ open group and from the different source (causes-of-death statistics). For the Marche, both sexes combined (T), the relative difference must stay within 3 % in every year both exist (2018–2021); the full table of differences goes into `results.md`.

### Education (`education.py`)

- **Enrolment** (`educ_uoe_enra11`): ISCED levels 0 to 8, by sex. Comparisons with Italy use an index with 2013 = 100, because absolute counts do not compare across areas of different size. The EU27 aggregate is incomplete in this dataset (2017 missing, several levels missing in other years), so enrolment is compared with Italy only; EU27 rows stay in the model table.
- **Indicators**, each shown with its EU 2030 target as a reference line:

  | Indicator | Source | EU 2030 target | Better when |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Early leavers from education and training, 18–24 | `edat_lfse_16` | below 9 % | lower |
  | Tertiary attainment, 25–34 (ISCED 5–8) | `edat_lfse_04` | at least 45 % | higher |
  | Early childhood education, age 3 to compulsory school age | `educ_uoe_enra22` | at least 96 % | higher |

- **Reliability:** the first two indicators come from the Labour Force Survey and rest on small regional samples. Eurostat flags (for example `u` low reliability, `b` break in series) are kept in the tables and drawn as hollow markers in charts. The text does not comment on year-to-year changes.

### Economy (`economy.py`)

- **GDP per inhabitant:** index in PPS with EU27 = 100 (`nama_10r_2gdp`, unit `PPS_HAB_EU27_2020`). It compares areas within a year. It is not a growth measure, and the methodology note says so, following Eurostat's guidance.
- **Unemployment rate, 15–74:** a single source, `lfst_r_lfu3rt`, for the regions, Italy and the EU27. The v0.1 report combined `tgs00010` (regions) with `tipsun20` (Italy and EU), two differently built series.

### Comparisons

For each indicator:

- the latest value, with the gap from Italy and from the EU27 (percentage points, or index points for GDP);
- the rank of the Marche among the six regions (1 = best, following `higher_is_better`; ties share the best rank);
- the change since the first year available (2013, or 2014 for early childhood education).

All comparisons use the latest year with a Marche value, for every geography.

## 3. Model and Power BI project

### Tables in `data/model/`

| Table | Grain | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `dim_geo` | geography | `geo_code`, `geo_name`, `geo_level` (nuts2, country, eu), `role` (focus, neighbour, benchmark), `sort_order` |
| `dim_year` | year | `year` (2013 to the latest year in any fact) |
| `dim_sex` | sex | `sex_code` (T, M, F), `sex_label` |
| `dim_isced` | ISCED 2011 level | `isced_code`, `isced_label`, `isced_group`, `sort_order` |
| `dim_indicator` | indicator | `indicator_code`, `indicator_name`, `unit`, `source_dataset`, `higher_is_better`, `eu_2030_target` (empty when none) |
| `fact_indicator` | geo × year × sex × indicator | `value`, `flag` — early leavers, tertiary attainment, early childhood education, GDP in PPS, unemployment |
| `fact_mortality` | geo × year × sex | `deaths`, `population_avg`, `crude_rate`, `std_rate`, `ci_low`, `ci_high`, `eurostat_std_rate` |
| `fact_mortality_5y` | geo × sex | `period_start`, `period_end`, `std_rate`, `ci_low`, `ci_high` |
| `fact_enrolment` | geo × year × sex × ISCED | `enrolled`, `flag` |

One long `fact_indicator` table lets DAX measures work for any indicator chosen in a slicer, instead of one copy of each measure per indicator. All relationships are one-to-many from a dimension to a fact, single direction; there are no bidirectional relationships.

Rows are sorted by key and each numeric column has a fixed rounding, so a diff in git always means changed data.

### Power BI project

- **Files:** `powerbi/marche-regional-stats.pbip`, the semantic model in TMDL (`marche-regional-stats.SemanticModel/definition/`) and the report in PBIR (`marche-regional-stats.Report/`). `.gitignore` excludes `.pbi/localSettings.json` and `.pbi/cache.abf`.
- **Power Query source:** a parameter `ModelSource` defaults to the raw GitHub URL of `data/model/` on `main`, so a clone opens and refreshes with no setup. For local work the parameter is set to the folder path. CSVs are parsed with culture `en-US`.
- **Measures** in a `_Measures` table, by display folder:
  - `Value`, `Latest Year (Marche)` (latest year with a Marche value for the selected indicator), `Value (latest)`;
  - `Change since first year`, `Rank among regions` (over the six regions);
  - `Distance to EU target` (sign follows `higher_is_better`), `Low reliability` (flag `u`, for conditional formatting);
  - mortality: standardised rate with interval, five-year rate, difference from Eurostat.
- **Calculation group `Comparison`:** items *Marche*, *Gap vs Italy*, *Gap vs EU27*. It replaces the duplicated v0.1 measures ("vs Italy", "vs EU", "vs Marche").
- **Pages:**
  1. Overview: Marche against Italy and the EU27 on every indicator, with distance to the EU targets.
  2. Mortality: crude against standardised rate (why standardisation matters), trend with intervals, Eurostat benchmark.
  3. Education: enrolment by ISCED level, indicators with EU target lines.
  4. Economy: GDP in PPS ranking, unemployment trend.

  Only tooltip pages with content are kept.
- **v0.1 reports:** `marche-demography-education.pbix` and `marche-regionali-2025.pbix` leave the tree; they remain in tag `v0.1.0`, and `powerbi/README.md` says so.

### Who builds what

Revised on 2026-10-09: the report exists to show the author's Power BI skill, so the author writes the DAX.

- Claude writes the scaffolding of the semantic model in TMDL: tables, columns, Power Query, relationships, the `ModelSource` parameter, and an empty `_Measures` table.
- The author writes every measure and the `Comparison` calculation group, builds the pages, and exports the PDF and screenshots, in Power BI Desktop (2025 or later, PBIP with TMDL and PBIR enabled).
- This happens in guided learning sessions (see the plan): Claude explains the concepts, asks questions, and reviews the author's work against the Python values, without writing the solutions.
- Two further sessions cover the statistical method (age standardisation, uncertainty and reliability), so the author can defend every number in an interview.

## 4. Error handling, testing, CI, documentation, release

### Error handling

- Every HTTP error raises an exception naming the dataset code and URL. Timeout as in v0.1 (180 s).
- After each download a coverage check requires the eight geographies and the expected years. The expected years of a dataset are the years with a Marche value. Known gaps are listed explicitly (EU27 deaths in 2024, EU27 enrolment in 2017). Any other gap stops the pipeline with a message naming the dataset and the geography.
- Eurostat missing values (`:`) become empty values with their flag kept. They are never imputed.

### Tests

Written before the code (TDD), on synthetic fixtures in the SDMX-CSV layout, as in v0.1.

- **Mortality:**
  - ESP weights sum to 100,000 and the 85+ weight is 2,500;
  - a hand-computed example with two age groups;
  - the Dobson interval starts at 0 with zero deaths and always contains the rate;
  - the redistribution of unknown-age deaths keeps the total;
  - the average population is the mean of two consecutive 1 January values;
  - the five-year rate is pooled, not averaged.
- **Education and economy:** flags are kept; index 2013 = 100, gaps and ranks are correct.
- **Coverage check:** a missing geography fails with a message naming it; an allowed gap passes.
- **Model contract:** columns and types of every table, one row per key, every fact key present in its dimensions.
- **TMDL contract:** the columns declared in the TMDL tables match the CSV headers in `data/model/`.
- **Benchmark on committed data:** for the Marche, `std_rate` and `eurostat_std_rate` in `data/model/fact_mortality.csv` differ by at most 3 %.

### CI

- `ci.yml` (every push and pull request): lint and tests as in v0.1, plus the new tests, including the contract and benchmark checks on the committed `data/model/`.
- `refresh.yml` (manual): downloads the data, regenerates `data/model/` and `docs/results.md`, and fails with the diff if the committed `data/model/` differs. `docs/results.md` is not compared, because it carries its generation date; it is uploaded as a workflow artifact. Regeneration is not run on every push, because it depends on the Eurostat service being available.

### Documentation

- `docs/methodology.md`: ESP 2013, Dobson interval, denominators, LFS reliability, PPS caveat, single unemployment source.
- `docs/results.md`: new generated sections.
- `powerbi/README.md`: model diagram, list of measures, how to open the project and set `ModelSource`.
- `powerbi/sources.md`: the v0.2 datasets.
- `docs/powerbi-checks.md`: manual cross-check of five DAX measures against the Python values.
- `README.md`: v0.2 summary, report screenshots.

### Release

Work happens on branch `v0.2`. Order:

1. Python pipeline (mortality, education, economy, model, report), with the two method sessions alongside.
2. TMDL scaffolding of the semantic model.
3. Power BI learning sessions: measures, calculation group, pages, cross-check.
4. PDF export and screenshots.
5. Version `0.2.0` in `pyproject.toml`, tag `v0.2.0`, GitHub release.
